The following section explores a variety of different computational methods that require explanation: topic modelling, concordances, word embedding models, and principal component analysis. Topic modelling is a computational linguistic method concerned with determining how related terms are within a specific text, with a focus on identifying what the text is about through the creation of topics. This method is less concerned with definitions of words, and more focused on the relational meaning of the words within the training data. The results of the method reveal the main categories, or topics, that are discussed in the training data. This method is effective at providing an overview of the topics of a text, but less effective at answering specific questions (such as how Muslims are represented).

Concordances show a term’s occurrences with its surrounding context, or to put it another way, they identify the words that surround a query term every time it appears in a text. There are a variety of tools that allow you to see concordances; this project uses AntConc for concordances (and collocates). This method is exclusively concerned with what words appear near the query term, so it does not attempt to make large claims about the meaning of words in a corpus. AntConc’s concordance tool shows every instance of a search word in its immediate sentence context. This is a useful method to better understand the context of each appearance of a term, as well as key word searching a collection of texts. The ‘Collocate’ tool in AntConc generates a list of words that frequently occur in the context of the search term. In other words, this tool allows users to investigate patterns in language. The tool provides the frequency of words appearing to the left or the right of the search query; this value measures how ‘related’ the search term and the collocate are. Collocates are like concordances, with the addition of statistical analysis. Collocates give statistical insights into the relationships seen in the concordances.

Word vectors were covered extensively in the introduction but will be glossed here. Word embedding models seek to map semantic proximity, which considers the meaning of the words within the specific context of the training corpus. Word vectors measure semantic proximity with cosine similarities. Cosine similarities are numerical representations of the ‘closeness’ between words in vector space. The larger the cosine number, the closer those two vectors are. Specifically, this method is identifying words that frequently appear in similar linguistic contexts (such as the linguistic context of days of the week like Monday and Saturday). 

A PCA plot for word embedding models visualizes high-dimensional word vectors in 2-D by reducing dimensions using Principal Component Analysis. Principal component analysis reduces the number of dimensions into principal components that retain most of the original information. PCA plots of word embeddings reveal semantic relationships through the creation of clusters of similar words. In theory, the center of a PCA plot represents the closest cosine similarities to the query terms. These plots display semantic proximity in a visual way, and it is meaningful what terms cluster around each other. In the case of a PCA plot, the closer the words are to each other in the visualization of vector space, the more contextually related they are.
